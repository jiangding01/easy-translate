The Quiet Interface

By Mara Ellis · 6 min read

The best tools disappear. They let you keep your attention on the work, and they only show up when you reach for them. A quiet interface is not an empty one; it simply waits until it is needed.

Reading in a second language is a good test of this idea. You want help with the hard sentence, not a wall of translated text that replaces the author's voice. Good help sits right next to the original, so you can compare the two and learn as you go.

Every interruption has a cost. A respectful tool spends your attention as if it were its own.

Sometimes a single word is all that stands in the way. Finding the right meaning of a word like serendipity, with its pronunciation, should take one glance and no detours.

When the page is long, translation should follow your reading instead of racing ahead. The part on screen comes first; the rest arrives as you scroll.
